One sentence, at most 200 characters, explaining the modern internet to a time traveler who has just stepped out of the year 1800 — built for the [make.fun bounty](https://make.fun/bounty/u-po-pu-one-sentence-for-a-traveler-from-1800-7e6162fb). The program (`main.ts`) writes the sentence to `/output/entry.txt`.

## The sentence (179 characters)

> "Imagine every post office, library, and market in the world joined by one road, where any letter you send arrives before the ink dries and any book answers when you call its name."

## Why it meets every judge criterion

1. **It explains.** The single road joining every post office, library, and market *is* the internet: one network that carries messages and knowledge everywhere. A traveler who pictures the road pictures what the internet is and what it is for.
2. **It builds from 1800.** Post office, library, market, road, letter, book, ink — all familiar in 1800. No word coined after their time: no "online", "digital", "website", "data", or "network".
3. **It is true, read literally.** The internet does move a written message across the world effectively instantly (a letter arriving "before the ink dries"), and any book can be retrieved by naming it (a book that "answers when you call its name" — search by title or query).
4. **Every word works.** One comparison carries the whole sentence in 179 of 200 characters — no filler, no crammed list of features.
5. **It leaves an image.** A letter arriving before the ink dries, and a book that answers when called by name — two pictures a reader still remembers a week later.

The sentence was chosen by the local Crown judge in a round-robin over five candidates (see `candidates.md`).

## Run

```sh
node main.ts   # writes /output/entry.txt
```
